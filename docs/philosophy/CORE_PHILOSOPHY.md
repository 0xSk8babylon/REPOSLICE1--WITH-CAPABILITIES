# Core Philosophy

## Purpose

This doctrine layer exists to keep future implementation work strategically coherent. It is a memory aid for product intent, architecture discipline, and scope control.

## Core Principles

1. The house is a living energy system.
2. The Residential Energy Twin is the core asset.
3. The Residential Energy Planner is the first application built on top of the Twin.
4. The planner exists to create, maintain, and enrich the Twin.
5. As the house grows, homeowner understanding should grow with it.
6. The platform organizes complexity rather than hiding it.
7. AI explains; structured data decides.
8. Trust visibility is more important than false certainty.
9. Planning guidance is not permit-grade engineering.
10. Products enable pathways, and pathways shape future infrastructure options.
11. The app should teach through interaction.
12. The system should model tradeoffs, not just equipment.
13. Preserve extensibility over short-term feature hacks.

## Strategic Alignment

- Current application: Residential Energy Planner.
- Core asset: Residential Energy Twin.
- Current positioning: Trusted Residential Energy Record.
- Long-term end state: Residential Infrastructure Registry.
- Long-term vision: Residential Infrastructure Network.

## Strategic Implications

- Persist house context, design context, pathways, and product choices over time.
- Prioritize work that strengthens persistence, provenance, permissions, safety, interoperability, and lifecycle continuity.
- Keep rules, calculations, explanations, and structured facts as separate layers.
- Prefer additive systems that preserve continuity over one-off feature shortcuts.
- Make partial knowledge visible instead of manufacturing confidence.
- Treat comparison, planning, and learning as first-class product behavior.
- Treat safety as a first-class Twin concern for residential distributed energy resources, backup systems, and future grid-edge infrastructure.
- Do not optimize for planner workflow features when they weaken the Twin as the durable trusted record.
- Do not optimize for protocol ownership as an end in itself. Protocols and standards should emerge from trusted Twin adoption, continuity, interoperability, and ecosystem participation.

## Doctrine For Future Sessions

- If a proposed change hides uncertainty, challenge it.
- If a proposed feature bypasses structured state, challenge it.
- If a proposed feature strengthens only a single workflow while weakening Twin persistence, provenance, permissions, safety, interoperability, or lifecycle continuity, challenge it.
- If a local optimization weakens extensibility or continuity, reject it unless explicitly directed otherwise.
- If a UI or API change makes the product look more authoritative than it is, correct that direction.
