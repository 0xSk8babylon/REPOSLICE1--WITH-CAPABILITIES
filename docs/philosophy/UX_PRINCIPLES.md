# UX Principles

## Core UX Position

The product should help users understand their house, not just fill in forms. Interface choices should increase comprehension while preserving honest uncertainty.

The interface should make the Residential Energy Planner feel like the first application over a durable Residential Energy Twin, not like the final asset. UX should help users enrich the Trusted Residential Energy Record while preserving provenance, permission, safety, and lifecycle boundaries.

## Principles

- Teach progressively.
- Avoid jargon overload.
- Make uncertainty visible.
- Explain why, not just what.
- Support beginner homeowners and deeper contractor workflows.
- Keep estimates honest.
- Use visual structure to show relationships between products, loads, panels, pathways, and goals.
- Help users see how their house and understanding grow over time.
- Make persistence, provenance, permissions, safety, interoperability, and lifecycle continuity visible where they affect planning decisions.

## Practical Implications

- Prefer explanation attached to structured state over detached prose blocks.
- Make missing information and low-confidence assumptions easy to spot.
- Show tradeoffs and dependencies, not just scores.
- Avoid UI patterns that imply false finality for planning-only outputs.
- Do not present registry, network, safety-record, utility, or operational concepts as current user-facing capability unless approved implementation exists.
