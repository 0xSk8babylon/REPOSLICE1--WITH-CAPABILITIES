# What We Are

Document role: external narrative. `VISION.md` defines principles. `PROJECT_MAP.md` documents current reality. `ARCHITECTURE.md` defines structure. `ECOSYSTEM_MAP.md` defines ecosystem relationships.

We are building a Customer-Permissioned Home Energy Graph that helps define, understand, and govern residential energy assets before they become operational grid assets. The company exists to give homeowners, contractors, utilities, suppliers, manufacturers, and future ecosystem participants a shared, permissioned source of truth for the energy reality inside and around the home.

# The Core Insight

The home is the grid edge.

Devices are the residential edge.

Utilities often see net load. They can see what the home consumes or exports at the meter, and they may see signals from enrolled devices or programs. But they do not always see the full residential asset truth behind the load.

They may not know the panel constraints, backup priorities, future EV plans, service limitations, contractor verification, homeowner intent, consent boundaries, or multi-device relationships that explain what is actually possible.

The platform exists to help define that truth.

# The Problem

Residential energy information is fragmented.

It is scattered across:

- utilities
- contractors
- installers
- manufacturers
- device apps
- spreadsheets
- proposals
- permits
- homeowner memory

Each participant sees a piece of the home. The utility may see the meter. The contractor may see the job. The installer may see the installed equipment. The manufacturer may see its own device. The homeowner may remember goals, constraints, future plans, and tradeoffs that no system records.

No single homeowner-governed record exists.

That fragmentation makes planning harder, increases repeated discovery, hides assumptions, weakens trust, and makes residential flexibility look simpler than it really is.

# Why Permission Matters

Technical capability alone is not grid capacity.

A battery, EV, generator, thermostat, solar system, or flexible load may be technically capable of creating value. But it only becomes usable grid capacity when the homeowner has allowed a specific use and the asset is actually available.

```text
Technical Capability
+
Customer Permission
+
Availability
=
Usable Grid Capacity
```

Customer willingness matters because homes are not anonymous grid equipment. They are lived-in places with comfort needs, backup priorities, safety concerns, privacy expectations, budgets, routines, and trust boundaries.

The grid cannot count a home as usable capacity simply because hardware exists. The homeowner's permission, preferences, and availability constraints determine whether that capability can be used.

# Category Definition

The category is Customer-Permissioned Home Energy Graph.

It means a homeowner-governed record of residential energy assets, constraints, plans, permissions, provenance, and relationships.

This category is different from dashboards because dashboards show information, but they do not become the durable truth layer for the home.

It is different from monitoring systems because monitoring systems observe behavior, while the graph preserves asset context, intent, permission, and evidence.

It is different from proposal tools because proposals sell or compare options, while the graph persists beyond a single offer.

It is different from contractor software because contractor software manages projects, while the graph manages residential energy truth.

It is different from DERMS because DERMS coordinate assets, while the graph defines the asset context before coordination.

It is different from HEMS because HEMS manage or optimize behavior inside the home, while the graph preserves the broader home energy record across devices, stakeholders, and time.
